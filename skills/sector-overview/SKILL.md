# Sector Overview

Explain how the sector works, where companies differ, and which evidence supports the conclusions. Adapt the scope and length to the user's question; default to a concise research note rather than a fixed-length report.

## Define a comparable scope

State product or service boundary, geography, time period, currency and scale, and whether the universe includes private companies. Match the depth and angle to the user's purpose. Use a reasonable stated scope when it can be inferred; clarify a missing choice only when it changes the analysis materially.

For supplied or synthetic material, identify it as such and keep conclusions within that evidence. Do not present a synthetic exercise as a real market estimate or investment conclusion.

## Map the market and value chain

Cover market size and growth where reliable evidence exists; distinguish historical measurements from forecasts and their assumptions. Identify customer groups, distribution, suppliers, barriers to entry, and sources of profit or bargaining power. Discuss structural drivers, cyclical exposure, technology, regulation, and consolidation where relevant.

Attach a source, date, scope, and method to each market-size claim. Distinguish total addressable market from the serviceable market and current sales. If a market denominator is absent, do not invent one or treat the revenue sum of selected peers as total market size. Avoid double counting revenue at different stages of the value chain. Use the correct interval count for CAGR.

## Compare relevant companies

Select peers by business model, product exposure, geography, scale, and economics; explain exclusions and material differences. Do not force a fixed number of competitors when the evidence supports fewer.

Use a compact table for available metrics such as comparable segment revenue, growth, operating or EBITDA margin, product exposure, and differentiator. Label the period, accounting definition, and source for each metric. Do not compare group revenue with a narrower segment without warning, or label gross margin as EBITDA margin.

Only calculate market share when numerator and denominator have compatible product, geography, period, and accounting coverage. A percentage of the supplied sample must be called a sample share, never market share. A partial dataset cannot establish an industry leader or concentration ratio.

Distinguish reported fact, company claim, estimate, and analyst inference. Include contradictory evidence and missing observations that might change the apparent competitive advantage.

## Add valuation context when supported

Compare current and historical multiples only with sourced dates and aligned periods. Separate trading multiples from transaction multiples and reported earnings from adjusted or forward estimates. Explain potential premium or discount drivers, including growth, margins, cyclicality, capital intensity, and balance-sheet risk.

If share prices, enterprise values, forecasts, or comparable ratios are absent, say the valuation comparison is unavailable. Do not derive stock returns or buy/sell rankings from revenue growth or operational data alone. Paid providers are optional; company filings and authoritative public material can support a bounded analysis.

## Deliver the useful conclusion

Default to a concise Markdown note with scope, the value-chain explanation, a peer comparison, key debates and catalysts, and explicit evidence gaps. Charts should clarify a supported relationship, not fill a quota. Provide an Excel appendix, Word document, or slides only when useful for the requested deliverable, using available host tools rather than assuming a specific Claude integration.

Date the analysis and flag stale observations. Explain what the evidence suggests, what remains uncertain, and which next source or observation would resolve the most consequential uncertainty. Do not send the report, purchase data, or place trades as part of this skill.
